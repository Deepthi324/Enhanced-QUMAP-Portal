Enhanced QUMAP: a hybrid fidelity–Euclidean distance for UMAP

Does blending a quantum-state-fidelity distance with Euclidean geometry give better patient embeddings than classical UMAP? This portal documents what the notebook actually implements, what each experiment shows, and what the paper can honestly claim.

Bottom line

The hybrid distance fixes original QUMAP and matches classical UMAP. It does not beat UMAP on classification under the leakage-safe protocols. Its consistent advantage is tighter class separation (silhouette), and it pays for that with slightly lower trustworthiness and about 3× the runtime.

Proposed

Enhanced (Hybrid) QUMAP cells 126–129, 145, 149, 157

The same UMAP settings with DH = α·DQ + (1−α)·‖x−y‖/s. The scale s is the median pairwise training distance in cells 126–145 and the mean in cells 149–177. α ∈ {0.25, 0.50, 0.75} was swept, and α = 0.50 was used for the final model and the graphs.

Mathematical formulation

The formulation is kept exactly as written in the notebook. The notes beside it describe properties the paper should state.

|ψx⟩ = x / ‖x‖Amplitude encoding of the standardised 30-D vector.
F(x,y) = |⟨ψx|ψy⟩|² = cos²θxyFor real vectors, fidelity is the squared cosine similarity.
DQ = 1 − F = sin²θxy
DE = ‖x − y‖ / s,  s = median or mean of training ‖xi−xj‖Train-only scale: s = 6.32 (median, 455 patients); s = 6.99 (mean, 500 patients).
DH = α·DQ + (1−α)·DE
Why fidelity alone fails (shown in the notebook). Cell 123 confirms DQ(x, −x) = 0. Opposite vectors count as identical, and magnitude is discarded. Measured consequences:
  • Rank correlation with Euclidean distance: ρ = 0.080 (cell 125)
  • Only 49% of top-10 neighbours agree with Euclidean (cell 114)
  • Same-class neighbours drop from 93.2% to 73.1% (cell 125)
  • Stage A SVM predicts every test patient benign (72/0, 42/0), so accuracy 63.16% equals the benign rate
The two terms are on different scales. DQ lies in [0, 1] with std 0.205. DE/mean spans 0 to 3.8 with std 0.478 verified here. At α = 0.50 the Euclidean term still dominates: the hybrid's 15 nearest neighbours overlap 81% with pure-Euclidean neighbours (Spearman 0.92). For α = 0.25 the figures are 90% and 0.99, and for α = 0.75 they are 71% and 0.61. So α does not mean "50% fidelity", and the paper should say so.
Metric status. 1 − F breaks the triangle inequality. UMAP's nearest-neighbour search tolerates this, but state it.

How the two splits relate verified here. The final 69 test patients (Stage F) are a strict subset of the Stage A 114 test patients. Complexity. Each method calls a Python-callable metric on every pair: O(n²·d) time, with no numba acceleration. That is why the hybrid costs about 6–7 s against about 2 s for UMAP (cell 128). It's trivial at n = 569 but won't scale without a numba-compiled metric.

What the numbers establish

  • Edges, degree, density: mostly fixed by construction. Minimum degree is 14 (= k − 1) in every graph, so the 3–4% differences are small by design.
  • Clustering 0.120 vs 0.112: the hybrid's neighbourhoods are slightly more closed (triangles), which fits its higher silhouette.
  • Max degree 38 vs 49, lower std: the hybrid graph is more evenly connected and less dominated by hubs.
  • Hub overlap: 3 of the top-10 hubs are shared between UMAP and Enhanced QUMAP (P075, P250, P325). Original QUMAP shares 1 with each, and no hub appears in all three.

What they don't establish

  • A hub is a patient in a dense region of the embedding. That says nothing about clinical importance.
  • Hub agreement between methods shows they agree with each other, not that either is correct.
  • No graph metric here uses the diagnosis. Adding edge homophily (the share of same-diagnosis edges) and the malignant share among hubs would tie the graphs to the task.

What is actually new

A split between what's established and what this work adds, so the claims survive review.

Existing / standard
  • UMAP with custom metrics
  • Amplitude encoding and pure-state fidelity (= cos² for real vectors)
  • Convex combinations of distances
  • QPCA-inspired = PCA (identical outputs)
  • Variational quantum autoencoders (here simulated and unconverged)
Defensible contribution
  • A quantified failure analysis of fidelity distance on standardised clinical data: sign invariance, ρ = 0.08 with Euclidean, 49% neighbour agreement, a 20-point drop in same-class neighbours, and collapse to all-benign predictions
  • A hybrid fix that restores UMAP-level accuracy, raises label separation by about 0.04–0.10 silhouette, and lowers fold variance
  • A characterised α trade-off: silhouette ↑ / trustworthiness ↓
  • A patient-graph comparison of how the distance reshapes connectivity
Claims to avoid. "Enhanced QUMAP outperforms UMAP" (not supported under CV or OOF), "quantum advantage", or "quantum speed-up". Everything runs classically, and the hybrid is about 3× slower.

Roadmap

These are minimal, focused changes, and none of them is applied yet. They wait for your approval.

Priority (strengthens the existing claims)

  • 1. Re-run the Stage E CV with one normalisation and the full grid α ∈ {0, 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1}. This puts UMAP and fidelity-only QUMAP into the same loop as built-in endpoints.
  • 2. Repeat that CV over the 5 seeds (5 × 5 = 25 runs per α) and add a paired Wilcoxon test vs UMAP.
  • 3. McNemar test on the existing all-569 OOF predictions (already in final_master_table).
  • 4. Add Enhanced QUMAP to the Stage A SVM benchmark so it's comparable with PCA and the AE.
  • 5. Record the α-selection rule.

Cheap ablations

  • Cosine distance (1 − cos θ, sign-aware) in place of fidelity. This tests whether the quantum squaring helps or hurts.
  • Scale matching: report the effective weight of each term, or rescale DE to [0, 1] as a variant.
  • Edge homophily for the three graphs.
  • Note the QAE's limits (100-sample training, unconverged) in Limitations rather than fixing them. It's not the paper's focus.

Research problem

Fidelity-only QUMAP collapses on standardised clinical data. Can a hybrid distance recover it?

Motivation

Quantum-inspired similarity is widely proposed, but its geometric failure modes on real tabular data are rarely measured.

Literature review

UMAP; quantum kernels and fidelity; QPCA; quantum autoencoders; metric combination; patient similarity networks.

Methodology

Amplitude encoding → DQ, DE, DH → UMAP with a callable metric (fit on train, transform test) → LR/SVM.

Formulation

F = cos²θ, sign and magnitude invariance, train-only scale s, effective weighting, metric properties.

Experimental setup

WDBC, fold-wise scaling, 5 seeds × 5 folds, α grid, metric definitions, software versions.

Results & discussion

Failure diagnosis, α trade-off curve, CV mean ± std with tests, graphs. The 69-patient split goes in as a supplement.

Limitations

One small dataset, classical simulation, O(n²) Python metric, no accuracy gain over UMAP, unconverged QAE.

Conclusion

The hybrid matches UMAP accuracy and improves label separation and stability. Fidelity alone is unsuitable.

Future work

More clinical datasets, numba-compiled metric, learned or adaptive α, hardware fidelity estimation.
